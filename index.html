The learning path I follow as a beginner

  1. Learn the vocabulary. I start with contract, tick, point, expiration, margin, and settlement, listed in the key terms below.
  2. Understand leverage. I remind myself that a small deposit controls a large contract value, which magnifies gains and losses equally.
  3. Practice on a simulator. I use a broker's free paper trading until my results are consistent and I follow my own rules.
  4. Write a trading plan. I define what I trade, when I enter, where I exit, and the most I will lose in a day.
  5. Start small. If I trade live, I begin with micro contracts and size positions so a single loss is a small share of my account.
  6. Review every trade. I keep a journal of what I planned, what happened, and what I would change.

Key terms

Tick and tick value

A tick is the smallest price move a contract can make. Tick value is the dollar amount of one tick. For ES, one tick is 0.25 points, worth $12.50.

Margin

Margin is a good-faith deposit your broker requires to hold a position. It is not a fee or a cost of the trade, and you can lose more than the margin you post.

Leverage

Leverage means controlling a large contract value with a small deposit. A small price move can cause a large change in your account balance.

Expiration and rollover

Each contract ends on a set date. Traders who want to keep a position open move ("roll") to the next contract before then.

Long and short

Going long means you profit if the price rises. Going short means you profit if it falls.

Stop order

A stop order closes a position when price reaches a level you choose, capping the loss on a trade. In fast markets the fill price can be worse than the stop.

Understand the risk first

I take this seriously: trading futures involves substantial risk of loss and is not suitable for every investor. Because of leverage, you can lose more than your initial deposit. Past performance does not indicate future results.
